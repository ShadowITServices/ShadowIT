# AZ-104: Microsoft Azure Administrator — 60-Day Socratic Study Program

- **Target exam:** [AZ-104](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/exams/az-104/) (skills measured as of 26 October 2023), leading to [Azure Administrator Associate](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/credentials/certifications/azure-administrator/)
- **Exam format:** 100 minutes, proctored, renews every 12 months
- **Time budget:** ~1.5 h/weekday, ~3 h/weekend day ≈ 100 h over 60 days
- **Assumed baseline:** Comfortable in a Microsoft 365 admin centre, some PowerShell, no prior Azure requirement
- **Video spine:** lecture numbers refer to Scott Duffy's *AZ-104 Microsoft Azure Administrator* course on Udemy. Using different material? Ignore them — the questions and the labs stand on their own.

## Why Socratic, and what that costs you

Most study plans are a reading list with dates attached. You watch, you nod, you
feel informed, and then the exam asks what happens to a resource lock when you move
the resource to another resource group and you discover that nodding wasn't knowing.

This plan inverts the order. **Every day opens with questions you answer before you
watch or read anything.** Then you go looking — not for the content, but for whether
you were right.

It is slower and more uncomfortable than watching lectures. That discomfort is the
mechanism: retrieving a wrong answer and then correcting it builds a far more durable
memory than reading the right one first. You are paying attention differently when
you already have a position to defend.

### The five rules

1. **Answer cold, in writing.** Before any video, any doc, any portal blade. A guess
   is fine. A blank is not — if you truly have nothing, write what you'd *expect* and
   why.
2. **Your answer is a hypothesis.** The lab is not a tutorial to follow; it exists to
   try to break what you just wrote down.
3. **"Because the course said so" is not an answer.** Every answer has to end in a
   mechanism — what Azure actually does — and where you observed it.
4. **Keep a question journal.** Four columns: the question, what you answered cold,
   what turned out to be true, and *why the gap*. That last column is the whole point.
5. **Three whys.** When you get an answer right, ask why three more times. Most
   AZ-104 scenario questions live two layers below the obvious answer.

<Admonition type="tip" title="Studying alone">

The Socratic method needs someone to push back. If you don't have a study partner,
say your answer out loud as if explaining it to a colleague who will ask "but why?" —
or hand your written answer to an AI assistant with the prompt *"Don't tell me if I'm
right. Ask me the question that exposes what I've got wrong."* Marking your own work
is the weak point of self-study; make something else do it.

</Admonition>

## Day 0 (before you start)

Set this up properly or you will spend Day 4 doing it badly.

1. **Subscription:** an [Azure free account](https://azure.microsoft.com/en-us/free/)
   (30-day credit plus 12 months of free services) or pay-as-you-go. A Visual Studio
   subscription credit works too.
2. **Budget and cost alerts:** set a [budget](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/tutorial-acm-create-budgets)
   on day zero with an alert at 50% and 80%. This is both lab hygiene and an exam
   objective you'll meet again on Day 13.
3. **Tooling:** [Azure PowerShell (Az module)](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/powershell/azure/install-azure-powershell),
   [Azure CLI](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/cli/azure/install-azure-cli),
   [VS Code](https://code.visualstudio.com/) with the Bicep and Azure Resource Manager
   Tools extensions.
4. **A disposable resource group per topic.** Name them `rg-az104-storage`,
   `rg-az104-network` and so on. Deleting a resource group is the only reliable way
   to stop paying for a lab you've forgotten.
5. **A second Entra tenant.** Free to create, and you'll need it for guest access and
   cross-tenant work in Week 2.
6. **The question journal.** A notebook, a text file, a OneNote section — anything
   with four columns. Set it up now; it is the single highest-value artefact of the
   next sixty days.
7. **Course lectures 1–14** (account, budget, service overviews) are orientation.
   Watch them today, not during a study day.

<Admonition type="warning" title="Spend control">

Bastion, VPN gateways, Application Gateway, Azure Firewall and premium storage bill
by the hour whether or not you're using them. Delete the resource group at the end
of any day that builds one. Set the budget alert first, not after the bill.

</Admonition>

## The daily loop

Every study day follows the same five-phase shape. The phases matter more than the
minutes — adjust the clock, keep the order.

| Phase | Time | What you do |
|---|---|---|
| **Prime** | 10 min | Read the day's questions. Write an answer to each, cold. No portal, no video, no search. |
| **Probe** | 40 min | Watch the lectures and read the linked docs. Mark every point where you were wrong. |
| **Prove** | 40 min | Do the lab. It is designed to test your answer, not to be followed blindly. |
| **Press** | 10 min | Answer the "press further" question out loud, in full sentences. |
| **Post-mortem** | 10 min | Fill in the journal. The *why the gap* column is mandatory. |

On weekends, run two days' material or use the extra time to redo a lab from memory
with the portal closed — PowerShell or CLI only.

## FOUNDATIONS — Days 1–3

### Day 1 — What Azure actually is when you click "Create"
*Lectures 15–17 · [Azure Resource Manager overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-resource-manager/management/overview)*

**Answer cold:**
- When you click **Create** in the portal, what receives that click? Is the portal talking to the VM service, or to something else?
- The portal, PowerShell, the CLI and an ARM template all produce the same VM. What does that tell you about where the real logic lives?
- What is a *resource provider*, and why would a deployment fail with "subscription not registered to use namespace"?
- Why is a resource group not a folder?

**Prove it:** Create a storage account in the portal. Before clicking Create, open
**Download a template for automation**. Now create the same account with
`az storage account create`. Compare the template to the command. Then run
`az provider list --query "[?registrationState=='NotRegistered'].namespace"` and
explain what that list is.

**Press further:** If ARM is the only way in, what does that imply about auditing —
where would you look to see every change anyone made to a subscription?

### Day 2 — Two CLIs and a context
*Lectures 18–19 · [Azure PowerShell](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/powershell/azure/what-is-azure-powershell) · [Azure CLI](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/cli/azure/what-is-azure-cli)*

**Answer cold:**
- Why does Azure ship two command-line tools rather than one? What is each actually good at?
- When you run `Connect-AzAccount`, what is stored, where, and for how long?
- You have access to four subscriptions. Which one does your next command target, and how would you know without running it?
- A command works in CLI but fails in PowerShell with "resource not found". Name three possible causes that have nothing to do with the resource.

**Prove it:** Sign in with both tools. Run `Get-AzContext` and `az account show`.
Switch subscriptions in each. Now deliberately break it: set your context to one
subscription and query a resource group in another. Read the error carefully — what
does it actually say, and what does it *not* say?

**Press further:** Why does the exam care whether you know the difference between
`az group create` and `New-AzResourceGroup` if both work?

### Day 3 — Scope: the idea everything else hangs off
*[Management groups](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/management-groups/overview) · [Subscriptions and tenants](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cloud-adoption-framework/ready/azure-best-practices/initial-subscriptions)*

**Answer cold:**
- Draw the hierarchy from management group down to a single resource. How many levels, and what sits at each?
- Can one Entra tenant hold many subscriptions? Can one subscription belong to many tenants? Why do those two questions have different answers?
- If you delete a subscription, what happens to the user accounts that had access to it?
- A permission is granted at the subscription and denied at the resource group. What wins, and why is that different from how NTFS works?

**Prove it:** In the portal, find the tenant ID and the subscription ID and note how
each is surfaced. Create a management group and move your subscription into it. Now
try to explain, without looking, where a policy assigned at the management group
takes effect.

**Press further:** Why can a resource group span regions but a resource cannot span
resource groups?

## DOMAIN 1 — Manage Azure identities and governance (20–25%) — Days 4–15

### Day 4 — Entra ID is not Active Directory
*Lectures 20–23 · [What is Microsoft Entra ID](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/fundamentals/whatis)*

**Answer cold:**
- Name three things AD DS does that Entra ID cannot do at all. Then three things Entra ID does that AD DS cannot.
- Entra ID has no OUs and no Group Policy. What replaces each?
- What protocol does a domain-joined PC use to authenticate to AD DS? What does a phone use to authenticate to Entra ID? Why can't they be the same?
- What do you actually get when you pay for Entra ID P1 or P2 that you don't get for free?

**Prove it:** Open your tenant's Overview blade. Find the tenant ID, the licence tier,
and the number of objects. Then attempt something P1-only (a dynamic group) and read
the error you get.

**Press further:** If Entra ID is not a domain controller, what is the thing that
makes "Entra joined" different from "domain joined" for a Windows device?

### Day 5 — Tenants, directories and the custom domain dance
*Lectures 24–29 · [Add a custom domain](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/fundamentals/add-custom-domain)*

**Answer cold:**
- Why does every new tenant get an `onmicrosoft.com` name, and can you ever delete it?
- To prove you own `example.com`, Microsoft asks for a TXT record. Why a TXT record and not a web page or an email?
- You add a custom domain and it stays "unverified". List four causes in order of likelihood.
- What is the practical difference between switching directories and switching subscriptions?

**Prove it:** Add a custom domain to your tenant (any domain you control — a cheap
`.au` is worth owning for lab work). Watch the verification fail before DNS
propagates, then succeed. Note how long it actually took.

### Day 6 — Users: more interesting than they look
*Lectures 30, 35 · [Manage users](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/fundamentals/how-to-create-delete-users)*

**Answer cold:**
- What is the difference between a member and a guest, expressed as a *permission*, not a description?
- A user's UPN and their email address differ. Is that broken? What breaks if it is?
- You delete a user by accident. How long do you have, and where do you go?
- Which user properties can a Helpdesk Administrator change, and which can they not?

**Prove it:** Create three users: one normal, one with a UPN that doesn't match their
mail, one you then delete. Recover the deleted one. Now bulk-create ten more from CSV
and deliberately malform one row — read how the bulk operation reports the failure.

**Press further:** Why does Microsoft treat "deleted" as a 30-day state rather than
an immediate removal? What attack does that defend against, and what attack does it enable?

### Day 7 — Groups and licensing, which are the same problem
*Lectures 31–32 · [Entra groups](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/fundamentals/concept-learn-about-groups) · [Group-based licensing](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/users/licensing-groups-assign)*

**Answer cold:**
- Security group or Microsoft 365 group — what decides, and which one can you use for an Azure RBAC assignment?
- Assigned, dynamic user, dynamic device: when is each the wrong choice?
- A dynamic group rule is `user.department -eq "Sales"`. A user moves to Sales. How long until they're in the group, and what runs to make that happen?
- A licence has a service plan the user's region can't have. What happens to the whole assignment?

**Prove it:** Build a dynamic group on `department`. Change a user's department and
time how long the membership takes to update. Then assign a licence to the group and
watch it flow to the member — then remove the user from the group and watch what
happens to their mailbox data.

**Press further:** Why is group-based licensing described as "eventually consistent",
and where does that bite you in a migration?

### Day 8 — Administrative units and devices
*Lectures 33–34 · [Administrative units](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/role-based-access-control/administrative-units) · [Device identity](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/devices/overview)*

**Answer cold:**
- An AU is often described as "like an OU". In what important way is that comparison wrong?
- You want a Perth helpdesk that can reset passwords only for Perth staff. What are the moving parts?
- Registered, joined, hybrid joined: which one does a personal iPhone get, and which does a corporate laptop get in a cloud-only org?
- What does "device writeback" mean, and why would an organisation need it?

**Prove it:** Create an AU, add users, scope a Helpdesk Administrator to it. Sign in
as that admin and try to reset a password for someone outside the AU. Read the error.

### Day 9 — External identities and self-service password reset
*Lectures 36–37 · [B2B collaboration](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/external-id/what-is-b2b) · [SSPR](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/entra/identity/authentication/concept-sspr-howitworks)*

**Answer cold:**
- When you invite a guest, where does their password live, and who can reset it?
- What can a guest see in your directory by default? Is that more or less than you'd assume?
- SSPR with one method or two? What is the actual security trade-off, not the policy recommendation?
- In a hybrid org, a user resets their cloud password. What has to be true for them to log in to their on-prem laptop afterwards?

**Prove it:** Invite a guest from your second tenant. Accept the invitation. Then, as
the guest, try to enumerate users in the host directory. Now tighten external
collaboration settings and repeat.

### Day 10 — RBAC: the anatomy of a role
*Lectures 38–39, 45 · [Azure RBAC overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/role-based-access-control/overview) · [Role definitions](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/role-based-access-control/role-definitions)*

**Answer cold:**
- A role assignment has three parts. Name them, then explain which one is hardest to get right in practice.
- What is the difference between `Actions` and `DataActions`? Give an example where someone has one but not the other.
- `NotActions` is not a deny rule. So what is it?
- Owner, Contributor, User Access Administrator: what exactly separates them?

**Prove it:** Open any built-in role's JSON in the portal. Find a role with
`DataActions` and one without. Then assign Reader to a test user at resource-group
scope and have them try to list storage keys. Explain the error in terms of the JSON
you just read.

**Press further:** Why does Azure need `DataActions` at all — why isn't a permission
just a permission?

### Day 11 — Control plane vs data plane, the distinction that trips everyone
*Lectures 40–42 · [Authorize access to blob data](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/authorize-access-azure-active-directory)*

**Answer cold:**
- You are Owner of a storage account. Can you read a blob inside it? Think carefully before answering.
- Why does "Storage Blob Data Reader" exist when "Reader" already does?
- An access key grants what, exactly, and to whom?
- Which of these is logged in the Azure Activity Log: creating a container, or uploading a blob?

**Prove it:** As Owner, switch the container's authentication method from access key
to Entra account in the portal and try to list blobs. Observe the failure. Grant
yourself Storage Blob Data Reader and retry. This one surprises almost everyone — write
down why it surprised you.

**Press further:** Given the above, what is wrong with the instruction "just make them
Owner, it'll work"?

### Day 12 — Custom roles, scope and inheritance
*Lectures 43–44 · [Custom roles](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/role-based-access-control/custom-roles) · [Scope](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/role-based-access-control/scope-overview)*

**Answer cold:**
- At what four scopes can a role be assigned? Which one can't be used for custom role *definitions*?
- Permissions inherit downward. Is there any way to subtract a permission further down the tree?
- You need "can restart VMs but not create or delete them". Which built-in role, or do you need a custom one?
- What is `AssignableScopes` for, and what happens if you set it too broadly?

**Prove it:** Write a custom role with `Microsoft.Compute/virtualMachines/restart/action`
and nothing else. Assign it. Verify the user can restart but not resize. Try to assign
the role at a scope outside `AssignableScopes` and read the failure.

### Day 13 — Subscriptions, management groups and the money
*Lectures 46–48, 56 · [Cost Management](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/cost-management-billing/costs/overview-cost-management)*

**Answer cold:**
- A budget and a cost alert sound like the same thing. What does a budget actually *do* when exceeded?
- Why can a management group tree be six levels deep but not sixteen?
- What does Azure Advisor base its cost recommendations on, and when should you ignore it?
- You need to split costs between two departments sharing a subscription. What are your options, and which is least painful?

**Prove it:** Build a management group hierarchy two levels deep. Create a budget with
an action group that emails you. Open Cost Analysis and group by resource group, then
by tag — note which one is currently useless to you and why.

**Press further:** If tags are the answer to cost allocation, what makes tags
unreliable in practice, and what feature fixes that? (You'll meet it tomorrow.)

### Day 14 — Azure Policy
*Lectures 50–52, 55, 58 · [Azure Policy overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/overview) · [Effects](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/governance/policy/concepts/effects)*

**Answer cold:**
- Policy and RBAC both stop people doing things. What is the actual difference?
- A policy with effect `Deny` is assigned. What happens to the twenty non-compliant resources that already exist?
- What is the difference between `Audit`, `AuditIfNotExists` and `DeployIfNotExists`?
- `DeployIfNotExists` has to create resources. Whose permissions does it use?

**Prove it:** Assign the built-in policy that restricts allowed locations. Try to
create a resource in a blocked region and read the error — note which layer refused
you. Then write a custom policy that denies storage accounts without a `CostCentre`
tag, and run a remediation task against existing resources.

**Press further:** Why is `Deny` evaluated at deployment time but compliance assessed
on a schedule? What does that gap mean for an auditor?

### Day 15 — Locks, tags, moving resources, and Domain 1 review
*Lectures 49, 53–54, 57 · [Resource locks](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-resource-manager/management/lock-resources) · [Move resources](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/azure-resource-manager/management/move-resource-group-and-subscription)*

**Answer cold:**
- `CanNotDelete` and `ReadOnly`: which one breaks more things unexpectedly, and why?
- You apply a lock at the resource group. Can you still delete a VM inside it? Can you still *stop* it?
- Do tags inherit from resource group to resource? If not, what makes people think they do?
- You move a VM to a new resource group. What is its resource ID afterwards, and what does that break?

**Prove it:** Lock a resource group `ReadOnly` and try to start a VM in it. (Think
about why that fails before you run it.) Then move a storage account between resource
groups and watch the validation step — note what it checks.

**Review:** 40 mixed Domain 1 questions. Update the journal. **Checkpoint:** can you
explain RBAC vs Policy vs locks, and control plane vs data plane, without notes? If
not, redo Days 10–14 before moving on.

## DOMAIN 2 — Implement and manage storage (15–20%) — Days 16–24

### Day 16 — Storage account anatomy and redundancy
*Lectures 59–61 · [Storage redundancy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/common/storage-redundancy)*

**Answer cold:**
- LRS, ZRS, GRS, GZRS, RA-GRS: order them by cost and by durability. Are those the same order?
- What does the "RA" in RA-GRS actually buy you that GRS doesn't?
- In a GRS account, how current is the secondary copy? What's the word Microsoft uses, and what does it cost you in a failover?
- Which redundancy options can you change after creation, and which require a migration?

**Prove it:** Create accounts with two different redundancy settings. Compare the
estimated monthly cost. Then attempt to change LRS to ZRS in the portal and record
exactly which conversions are offered.

**Press further:** Why does ZRS protect against a datacentre fire but not a regional
outage, when GRS protects against both? What are you actually paying for?

### Day 17 — Access tiers and lifecycle management
*Lectures 62, 73 · [Access tiers](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/access-tiers-overview) · [Lifecycle management](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/lifecycle-management-overview)*

**Answer cold:**
- Hot, cool, cold, archive: what two numbers move in opposite directions as you go down that list?
- A blob in archive is needed right now. What do you do, and how long does it take?
- There's an early-deletion penalty on cool and archive. Why would Microsoft charge that?
- Tier can be set on the account and on the blob. Which wins?

**Prove it:** Upload a file, set it to archive, then rehydrate it and time the
operation. Write a lifecycle rule that moves blobs to cool after 30 days and deletes
them after 365 — then work out when it next runs.

### Day 18 — Storage networking
*Lectures 63 · [Storage network security](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/common/storage-network-security)*

**Answer cold:**
- A storage account has a public endpoint by default. Is "public" the same as "anonymous"?
- Service endpoint vs private endpoint: what changes about the traffic path, and what changes about DNS?
- You restrict an account to one VNet. What breaks for you, the administrator, sitting at home?
- What are "trusted Microsoft services" and why does the exception exist?

**Prove it:** Lock an account down to a VNet. Confirm you're locked out from your own
machine. Add your IP as an exception. Then add a private endpoint and run `nslookup`
on the account's FQDN from inside and outside the VNet — compare the answers.

**Press further:** Why does a private endpoint need a private DNS zone? What exactly
would break without it?

### Day 19 — Data protection and encryption
*Lectures 64–66 · [Data protection overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/data-protection-overview) · [Storage encryption](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/common/storage-service-encryption)*

**Answer cold:**
- Soft delete, versioning, snapshots and point-in-time restore all protect blobs. What is each one actually for?
- Storage is encrypted at rest and you can't turn it off. So what is a customer-managed key *for*?
- If you hold the key in Key Vault and delete the key, what happens to the data?
- What does an immutability policy prevent that a resource lock doesn't?

**Prove it:** Enable soft delete and versioning. Delete a blob and recover it. Overwrite
a blob and recover the previous version. Then set a legal hold and try to delete the
container.

### Day 20 — Blobs, files, queues and tables
*Lecture 67 · [Storage services](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/common/storage-introduction)*

**Answer cold:**
- Four services, one account. What decides which you'd use?
- Block blob, append blob, page blob: which one backs a VM disk, and why that one?
- When would you choose Azure Files over Blob storage for something that "is just files"?
- What does a queue give you that a table doesn't?

**Prove it:** Create one of each in a single account. Upload the same file to blob and
to a file share — compare how each is addressed, and how each is accessed from a VM.

### Day 21 — Access keys and SAS
*Lectures 68–69 · [SAS overview](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/common/storage-sas-overview)*

**Answer cold:**
- An account has two access keys. Why two, not one or three?
- Account SAS, service SAS, user delegation SAS: which is most secure and why?
- A SAS has leaked. What are your options, and what does each one break?
- What is a stored access policy for, given SAS tokens already have an expiry?

**Prove it:** Generate a read-only SAS for one blob with a 10-minute expiry. Use it in
a browser. Let it expire and read the error. Now generate one tied to a stored access
policy and revoke it mid-life — note how fast revocation takes effect.

**Press further:** Rotating an access key invalidates every SAS signed with it. What
does that tell you about how a SAS is validated — does Azure store the tokens it issues?

### Day 22 — Entra ID authorisation for storage
*Lecture 70 · [Authorize with Entra ID](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/blobs/authorize-access-azure-active-directory)*

**Answer cold:**
- Why is RBAC-based access to blob data considered safer than a SAS?
- You can disable shared key authorisation entirely. What breaks when you do?
- Which identities can be granted data-plane roles? Can a VM be one?
- How does the portal decide whether to show you blobs via key or via your account?

**Prove it:** Disable shared key access on a test account and find out which of your
tools stop working. Then grant a VM's managed identity Storage Blob Data Contributor
and read a blob from inside the VM with no credentials in the code.

### Day 23 — Moving data: AzCopy, Storage Browser, object replication
*Lectures 71–72, 74–76 · [AzCopy](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/common/storage-use-azcopy-v10)*

**Answer cold:**
- AzCopy, Storage Explorer, the portal, `az storage blob upload`: when is each the right tool?
- AzCopy can authenticate two ways. Which would you use in a scheduled script, and why not the other?
- Object replication copies blobs between accounts. What has to be enabled on both for it to work?
- Is object replication a backup? Defend your answer.

**Prove it:** Copy a folder with AzCopy using a SAS, then again using `azcopy login`.
Set up object replication between two accounts and measure the lag.

### Day 24 — Azure Files, snapshots, and Domain 2 review
*Lectures 77–78 · [Azure Files planning](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/storage/files/storage-files-planning)*

**Answer cold:**
- SMB and NFS shares: what decides which you can use, and what must be true of the account?
- To mount a file share from a domain-joined PC, what authentication options exist?
- A share snapshot is taken. Where does it live, and what does it cost?
- Why would you use Azure File Sync rather than just mounting the share?

**Prove it:** Create a file share, mount it from a VM, take a snapshot, delete a file,
restore it from the snapshot.

**Review:** 40 mixed Domain 2 questions. **Checkpoint:** can you explain SAS types,
redundancy options, and the difference between soft delete and versioning cold?

## DOMAIN 3 — Deploy and manage Azure compute resources (20–25%) — Days 25–40

### Day 25 — Creating a VM: every decision on the blade
*Lectures 79, 82 · [VM sizes](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/virtual-machines/sizes)*

**Answer cold:**
- The create-VM blade asks maybe thirty questions. Which five can you never change afterwards?
- What does the VM size letter actually mean — why is a D-series not just "bigger than B"?

## If you fall behind

You will. The plan has no slack in it by design — slack gets absorbed silently and you
find out in week eight.

When you lose a day, **do not compress two days into one.** Drop the Probe phase on the
easiest upcoming day and keep the Prime and Prove phases. A day where you answered
questions and ran a lab without watching the videos is worth more than two days of
videos at double speed.

The order to sacrifice things, worst case: Press, then Probe, then the lab. Never the
Prime questions, and never the journal — those are the plan.
